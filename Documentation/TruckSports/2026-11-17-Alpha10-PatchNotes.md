## New Sports

### Water Sports

* **Truck Canoeing: Paddle Pals:** Two trucks, one canoe, and a river with no interest in your friendship. Independent paddle stations apply real off-center impulses. Matching your partner's cadence earns synchronization; uneven strokes turn the hull. Currents, collisions and capsizes complicate the journey. AI partners use the same paddling rules.
* **Truck Kayaking: Whitewater Wipeout:** A semi has been issued a kayak. The equipment department refuses further comment. Alternating strokes, water steering, lean, bracing, rolls and measured airborne landings distinguish this craft from the canoe. Leaving the actual water produces a stranded outcome rather than an endless floating shortcut.
* **Blackwater: Something Below:** The freight is waiting across the midnight lake. So is something considerably larger. Contact-collected supply crates and an underwater rigidbody encounter share the existing buoyancy foundation. Real wakes, lift, sideways forces and roll torque put the threat in the water, not merely on the HUD.

### Horror Encounters

* **Graveyard Shift: The Last Stop:** Check the pumps. Inspect the utility room. Answer the CB task. A routine shift develops disappearing trucks, blackouts and an apparition. Contextual jobs unlock progression and extraction; new source adds real carried supply parcels and supported shelf placement, with a remaining parcel-navigation defect under repair. A complete restaurant/store activity loop remains unfinished.
* **Truck Harvest: Scarecrow Season:** The harvest festival has a strict closing policy. Restore wards, navigate changing corn passages and evade a stalker that responds to observation, engine noise and headlights. Eliminated drivers can follow surviving participants.
* **Dead End: The Last Headlight:** The next exit looks familiar. So did the last three. Ordered road objectives, contradictory signals and a physical pursuing semi form the candidate's highway escape. Puzzle and survival variants retain separate rules.
* **Cursed Cargo:** The trailer has several complaints about its destination and has chosen to submit them through the fifth wheel. Haunted brakes, shifting cargo, sway and door/light events accompany real containment stops and delivery. Shared receiving bays now have AI queuing and a physical reverse departure procedure.

### Trailer Physics

* **Trailerblazers: Rocket Launch:** Launch control has approved the trailer as a booster stage. Actual thrust consumes fuel; physical separation changes the moving assembly. Descent control gives the tractor a fighting chance of landing. Altitude, distance and flight measurements come from the run.
* **Jackknife Highwire:** Narrow elevated freight roads, articulated loads and crosswind. The trailer receives no exemption from gravity when the tractor turns. Gates, cargo condition and actual arrival determine the result.
* **Hitch & Run:** Line up the kingpin, lock the native fifth wheel and deliver the cargo. A steal requires an explicit stopped physical contest and recoupling. Touching another truck does not transfer ownership.
* **Trailer Trebuchet:** Medieval logistics has entered its difficult phase. A physical loading ramp, counterweight, hinged arm and releasable point sling launch the original trailer. Earlier loading/throw evidence does not certify the changed siege apparatus. Retractable loading bridges, measured release timing and separate human driving/aiming stages are implemented; useful downrange flight and castle impacts remain under focused acceptance.
* **Hitch Handoff:** The trailer is the baton. Incoming drivers stop, uncouple and clear the pin while the next teammate physically positions and recouples. Team results follow the actual relay.
* **Unhitched!:** Balance an unjointed tractor on a physical flatbed. Native front rolling supports keep the loaded nose off its parking stand. Single-driver and actual four-driver moving-platform rounds now pass support/scoring/rematch checks. Alternate ramp and trick acceptance remains open.
* **Cargo Crisis:** A roof-corner winch connection provides real righting leverage, followed by reverse alignment, native coupling and a stopped depot delivery. Cable contact alone earns nothing.
* **Domino Derby:** Stand full-size trailers upright, edit the layout, then push the first piece. Actual collision contacts carry the chain. Unrelated falls cannot silently join the scoring chain. The overhead camera follows the real collapse. New source records a bounded collapse replay across all four actual chains, with collider-free ghosts and unchanged scores; actual four-chain playback and rematch passed AdvancedMechanics60; creative-layout features remain unfinished.
* **Trailer Plinko:** Pick a release position, angle, tilt and spin, then let inertia and physical pegs settle the argument. New source supports configurable multi-drop matches; three real drops and cumulative scoring have passed focused testing. The full-size board uses collision shapes matching the visible pegs. Points require supported rest in a real catch tray.
* **Jackknife on Ice:** Drive traction and sideways ice grip are tuned separately through NWH's wheel APIs. Controlled lateral travel, precision gates and recovered physical spins supply the candidate scoring. Hitch, support and speed guards remain intact.
* **Road Train Wreck:** Physically connected trailers introduce delayed reactions, sway, braking and genuine chain failures. New source retains the default three and provides an eight-trailer authored capacity with a six-trailer Maximum challenge; native three-to-eight-to-three links and an actual six-trailer delivery/rematch passed AdvancedMechanics60; four long simultaneous human trains remain unverified. Surviving connections matter. A detached mesh parked nearby does not count as part of the train.
* **Hitch Ring Toss:** Build momentum, release the native hitch and send the entire trailer through a physical aperture. Whole-body clearance is required. Retracted flight legs and properly positioned launch equipment support a real throw instead of a projected scoring guess.
* **Zero-G Hitch-Up:** Wheel propulsion is disabled in orbit. Six-degree-of-freedom thrusters, fuel, rotational inertia and velocity matching lead to a native hitch and slow cargo delivery. A broken joint invalidates possession even when road-driving updates are disabled.
* **Skyhook Sprint:** Suspended freight hangs from a physical cable and constrained trolley. Acceleration, braking, swing, wind and release timing affect its landing. Slalom variants use authored openings; sprint delivery requires the load upright and settled.
* **Ice Road Reckoning: Thin Ice:** Thickness, supported vehicle loads and accumulated stress determine cracking and breakthrough. Heavy cargo, changing traction and depot braking make route choice matter. Losing the actual support collider can send freight below the lake.
